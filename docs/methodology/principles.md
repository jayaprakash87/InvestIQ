# Investment Research Methodology — Principles v0.1

This is a methodological specification, not a validated investment strategy.

## Distinct analytical lenses
1. **Business quality:** earning power, cash conversion, balance sheet, reinvestment economics, moat.
2. **Fundamental trajectory:** revenue, margins, cash flow, revisions, competitive changes.
3. **Current valuation:** normalized expectations, scenario returns, downside and uncertainty.
4. **Short-horizon catalysts:** testable events and market expectations; not guaranteed predictions.
5. **Long-horizon optionality:** credible earnings pathways, reinvestment runway, dilution and terminal value.
6. **Portfolio context:** risk factors, correlated exposures, allocation and transaction costs.

Business quality, investment attractiveness *at the current price*, and speculative upside are separate results. No universal ranking or factor weights are assumed valid across industries.

## Source and time discipline
Every material numeric input should carry a security identifier, units/currency, fiscal period, source, retrieval time, publicly-available-as-of time where known, and a missing/estimated/reported classification. Store raw observations separately from normalized metrics and model assumptions.

## Scenario discipline
Report bear/base/bull inputs with reasons. Expected return estimates are conditional on assumptions. Explain limitations for negative earnings, cyclical peak estimates, dilution, debt, tax and FX. Do not infer statistical probabilities from editorial scenarios.

## Different economic engines
Semiconductors, optical connectivity, industrials, utilities, software and financial companies need distinct business drivers, metric choices and valuation methods. Avoid comparing irrelevant ratios.

## Validation safeguards
Require point-in-time constituents and data, surviving and delisted securities, report-publication delays, commissions/slippage, benchmark choice, model-version history, out-of-sample evaluation and exposure/risk attribution. Historical backtests never equal realized live returns.
